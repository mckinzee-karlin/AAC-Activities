Reading isn't one single skill — it's the product of two of them working together. This is known as the Simple View of Reading.

Because this is a multiplication relationship rather than an addition, both pieces matter. A student can be strong in one area and still end up with weak reading comprehension if the other area is weak. Let's see what that looks like.

Sometimes students do not know the code and therefore cannot read even if they know what all the words mean.

Sometimes, students have to work hard to sound out words, which slows down fluency and limits comprehension.

Even though you were able to sound out every word in "The glorp daxed the flinder before it splinked into the gard," you probably struggled with the comprehension questions — because the sentence is made up of nonsense words.

Sometimes students can decode every word and still not understand the message, because comprehension is language-based, not just decoding-based.

Notice something?

You just completed several activities without using any speech at all. Instructions were shared through writing, and you understood them through reading.

For students to learn to read with comprehension, they must:

  • Recognize written words / know how to sound them out
  • Understand the meaning of the words they read
    • Some students struggle with word recognition and decoding
    • Some students struggle with language comprehension
    • Some students struggle with both
  • Students with communication disorders can learn these skills

AAC does not hinder speech production

  • There is no research to show that using AAC will hinder speech production.
  • Sometimes, using AAC can help students increase their speech.
  • Sometimes, students who use AAC will go on to use speech as their main communication modality. Sometimes, they will not.
  • Communication takes many forms for all of us. Speech is only one.
  • If a student is not speaking, they still require opportunities to learn language skills.
  • It is impossible to teach literacy without language, but it is entirely possible to teach language without speech.
