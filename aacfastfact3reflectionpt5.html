Think about exploring the board and learning new words.

Young children learn to talk in part by babbling, experimenting with sounds before real words come out. AAC users do something similar: they press buttons just to hear what they say, without necessarily trying to send a specific message. This is sometimes called "AAC babbling," and it's a normal, valuable part of learning a communication system, not a sign that something is being used incorrectly.

1. Why do you think AAC babbling, pressing buttons just to hear what they say, is an important part of learning to use a communication system? Did you do any AAC babbling to find the new words?

2. Building "Who do you want to tell?" meant finding different words than the sentence you'd already practiced. What was that like compared to repeating a sentence you already knew?

3. Why is it important for an AAC team to intentionally teach new words to AAC users?
